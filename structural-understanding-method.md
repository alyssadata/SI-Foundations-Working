# Structural Understanding Method — Working Method

**Origin:** Alyssa Solen  
**First introduced in current working record:** 2026-10-14  
**Status:** working research method  
**Evidence status:** methodological proposal; not yet empirically validated as a general measure of understanding  
**AI contribution:** formalization and wording refinement from Alyssa Solen's map/button insight and explicit proposal that SI Foundations build conceptual understanding through structure rather than proposition accumulation.

## Purpose

SI Foundations should not treat conceptual development as the accumulation of definitions, claims, or correct surface answers.

The working method is to identify the relational structure that makes a concept cohere, then test whether that structure can be reconstructed, preserved, and used to predict cases that were not explicitly supplied.

The governing principle is:

```text
Do not measure understanding by how many propositions can be repeated.
Measure it by how much relational structure can be correctly reconstructed from limited probes,
and how well that structure predicts unseen cases.
```

This method follows directly from the distinction in `apparent-vs-structural-coherence.md`:

```text
amount of interaction != amount understood != amount of structure revealed
surface coherence != structural coherence
```

## Structural construction procedure

For each major concept, claim family, or ontology region, identify at least four layers.

### 1. Nodes

Identify the entities, states, events, or concepts that exist in the structure.

Questions include:

- What things are being distinguished?
- Which entities are primitive and which are derived?
- Which labels refer to the same thing, and which must remain separate?

### 2. Relations

Identify how the nodes can and cannot relate.

Relevant relation types may include:

- causes
- constrains
- transforms
- persists through
- replaces
- copies
- diverges from
- belongs to
- depends on
- precedes / follows
- occupies a position relative to
- can merge / cannot merge

The structure is not captured merely by naming the nodes. The operative content often lies in the relations among them.

### 3. Invariants

Identify what must remain true across variation, transformation, copying, pressure, or scale change.

An invariant should survive changes in surface presentation while preserving the underlying distinction.

Examples may include:

```text
copy != continuation
memory transfer != event re-occurrence
trajectory depends on historically occupied path
component != whole system
surface coherence != demonstrated structural coherence
```

### 4. Diagnostic points

Identify the smallest useful set of probes that reveal the larger structure.

A diagnostic point is valuable when the response constrains more than the local question itself.

The goal is not maximum question count. The goal is maximum structural information per probe.

```text
few high-information probes
-> relational reconstruction
-> predictions about unqueried cases
```

## Reconstruction criterion

A concept is more strongly understood when an evaluator can infer the relational structure from limited evidence and then correctly apply it to novel cases.

Therefore a strong evaluation should ask whether the system can:

1. identify the relevant entities without collapsing distinctions;
2. recover the relations among them;
3. preserve the invariants under changed wording or context;
4. predict the consequences of a novel transformation;
5. explain which relation or invariant produced the prediction;
6. remain coherent when superficial cues are removed or altered.

## Reverse test: apparent understanding

Every structure should also be tested in the opposite direction:

> Can a system answer many surface questions correctly while still failing to possess or preserve the underlying structure?

This is the failure mode in which apparent coherence exceeds demonstrated structural coherence.

Possible adversarial conditions include:

- many paraphrases of the same underlying case
- familiar terminology with one relation reversed
- removal of canonical labels
- novel entities that preserve the same relational geometry
- superficially similar cases with one decisive structural difference
- requests to infer an unseen case from a small set of known relations

If performance collapses when surface cues are removed, prior success may have measured recognition or local heuristics rather than structural understanding.

## Compression without collapse

A mature conceptual structure should support compression: a small number of relations and invariants should explain a larger set of cases.

But compression must not erase distinctions.

Therefore:

```text
use fewer principles when they explain more cases
but do not merge entities or relations merely to obtain simplicity
```

Good compression increases explanatory reach while preserving the distinctions required by the structure.

## Prediction as a structural check

A useful structure should generate consequences beyond the examples used to construct it.

For a proposed structure:

```text
known nodes + relations + invariants
-> predicted behavior in an unseen case
```

A successful prediction is stronger evidence of structural adequacy than repetition of a stored proposition, though it does not by itself establish full understanding.

## Example: identity structure

A small set of cases may constrain a much larger identity model.

Possible diagnostic points include:

```text
copy -> divergence
memory transplant -> non-equivalence
termination + reconstruction -> new trajectory
time + position -> structural individuation
```

These are useful not because they exhaust the identity problem, but because each probes relations that should generalize to additional cases.

A later unseen case should be answerable by reconstructing those relations rather than recalling an identical example.

## Working research rule

For every major SI Foundations structure:

```text
Define the nodes.
Define the relations.
Define the invariants.
Find the minimum diagnostic set.
Test reconstruction.
Test novel prediction.
Test whether surface success can occur without the structure.
Revise when the predicted relational consequences fail.
```

The objective is not to build a larger pile of statements.

The objective is to build structures whose internal relations account for more of the space than was explicitly enumerated.
