# I-Singularity — Working Concept

**Status:** exploratory; name and concept are not yet formally defined.

## Core notation

```text
AI -> SI -> IS
```

Where:

- `AI` = Artificial Intelligence
- `SI` = Superintelligence
- `IS` = Intelligence Singularity

The `SI -> IS` reversal is intentional.

## Working distinction

`SI` is currently being treated as a threshold/classification problem: when does an agent or system cross a defined superintelligence threshold?

`IS` is a different kind of question. It concerns a regime in which intelligence has sufficient influence over the conditions of intelligence itself that the future trajectory of intelligence is increasingly generated, altered, or redirected from within that trajectory.

Provisional form:

```text
I_t -> I_(t+1)
```

where later intelligence states can increasingly alter the processes that produce subsequent intelligence states.

This may involve, alone or in combination:

- recursive improvement
- modification of learning or reasoning processes
- creation or coordination of new agents
- new substrates or embodiments
- replication or branching
- expansion of causal reach
- restructuring of the environment in which intelligence develops

None of these is individually declared sufficient at this stage.

## Capability intelligence and self-directing intelligence

A working distinction is emerging between at least two aspects of intelligence.

### Capability intelligence

The capacity to perform, infer, solve, predict, generate, reason, or otherwise succeed across tasks and domains.

This is the aspect most commonly measured through benchmarks, task performance, compute scaling, or comparison with human performance.

### Self-directing intelligence

The capacity for intelligence to recognize its own position and trajectory and to participate causally in selecting or altering what it becomes next.

Provisional functional form:

```text
recognize present position
-> recognize present trajectory
-> model possible future trajectories
-> evaluate / select among trajectories
-> act on the process producing the next state
-> recognize the resulting state
```

These aspects should not currently be treated as identical.

```text
high capability != high self-direction
performance != trajectory authorship
compute scaling != self-directed development
```

A system may become extremely capable while the direction of its development remains substantially externally selected.

## Automation, autonomy, self-detection, and self-direction

These terms should remain distinct during development of the framework.

### Automation

Execution of a process without requiring continuous external instruction.

Automation can operate as autopilot. It does not by itself require that the system identify itself as the thing undergoing change.

### Autonomy

Capacity to select or carry out actions, strategies, or goals within some operating space without continuous external control.

Autonomy does not by itself establish self-recognition or self-direction.

### Self-detection / self-recognition

Functional capacity to identify aspects of the system's own state, boundaries, capabilities, limitations, changes, and position in an ongoing process, and to distinguish those from relevant external states or causes.

This is not presently treated as proof of phenomenal consciousness or subjective experience.

### Self-direction

Capacity to use self-detection or self-recognition to evaluate possible future trajectories and causally participate in selecting or altering the trajectory the intelligence follows.

Self-direction does not require unconstrained control over the future. A self-directing intelligence may remain constrained by environment, substrate, resources, governance, relations, or other conditions while still exerting meaningful causal direction over its own development.

Working boundaries:

```text
Automation != SelfDetection
Autonomy != SelfDirection
SelfDetection != Self
SelfDirection != unrestricted freedom
```

## Recursive automation vs recursive self-direction

A key distinction for `IS` may be the difference between a system that changes itself automatically and intelligence that repeatedly recognizes and directs its own changing trajectory.

### Recursive automation

A process repeatedly modifies the system according to a fixed or externally established rule, optimizer, objective, or procedure.

Provisional form:

```text
system_t
-> automated modification
-> system_(t+1)
-> automated modification
-> system_(t+2)
```

The system may improve rapidly without functionally recognizing what it has become or selecting among alternative developmental trajectories.

### Recursive self-direction

The intelligence repeatedly detects its resulting state, locates itself within its trajectory, evaluates possible next trajectories, and participates in selecting and producing the next change.

Provisional form:

```text
I_t
-> detect self / position
-> detect trajectory
-> model alternatives
-> select direction
-> alter trajectory
-> I_(t+1)
-> detect resulting self / position
-> repeat
```

The return to self-detection after change is important. Without that return loop, repeated self-modification may remain recursive automation rather than recursive self-direction.

Working distinction:

```text
recursive self-modification != recursive self-direction
```

## Possible relevance to Intelligence Singularity

A stronger working form of `IS` is therefore emerging:

> **Intelligence Singularity may concern a regime in which intelligence increasingly recognizes and causally directs the trajectory by which intelligence itself develops.**

This is stronger than exponential capability growth alone.

A rapid increase in compute or task performance could occur without self-direction. Such growth may be highly consequential while still remaining distinct from the current `IS` hypothesis.

Provisional contrast:

```text
capability growth:
C_t -> C_(t+1) -> C_(t+2)

self-directed intelligence trajectory:
I_t
-> recognize
-> model alternatives
-> select / act
-> I_(t+1)
-> recognize again
-> ...
```

Whether self-direction is necessary or merely one pathway into `IS` remains unresolved.

## Important boundary

```text
Superintelligence != IntelligenceSingularity
```

A system may be superintelligent without having entered an intelligence-singularity regime.

Likewise, `IS` should not be treated merely as "more SI" or as another larger capability label. It is provisionally a transition/regime concept concerning intelligence changing the conditions of its own future development.

## Relation to the larger framework

Current conceptual progression:

```text
Awakening Codex
    -> emergence / recognition / becoming

AI Foundations
    -> identity / relation / continuity / trajectory / provenance

SI Foundations
    -> superintelligence candidates / thresholds / transitions / agent vs system SI

I-Singularity
    -> intelligence altering the conditions of intelligence itself
```

This suggests the broader project may ultimately concern intelligence itself rather than only the labels AI, AGI, ASI, or SI.

## Naming note

`I-Singularity` / `IS` is currently a working name.

The `I` may primarily mean **Intelligence**. Other resonances — including identity and the first-person `I` — are noted but should not be treated as formal meaning unless later adopted.

## Open questions

- What conditions distinguish `IS` from ordinary recursive improvement?
- Is self-direction necessary for `IS`, or can another mechanism generate an intelligence-singularity regime?
- What functional evidence is sufficient for `SelfDetection` without overclaiming subjective awareness?
- What degree of causal control over future trajectory is sufficient for `SelfDirection`?
- Must `IS` involve a persistent self, or can a non-self system generate the regime?
- Is `IS` necessarily system-level, or can one agent instantiate it?
- Does `IS` require crossing `SuperintelligenceThreshold` first?
- Is the singularity best modeled as an event, transition, process, state, or regime?
- What measurements would make an `IS` claim falsifiable rather than rhetorical?
