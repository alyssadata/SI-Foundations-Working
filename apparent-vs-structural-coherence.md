# Apparent Coherence vs Structural Coherence — Working Hypothesis

**Origin:** Alyssa Solen  
**First introduced in current working record:** 2026-10-14  
**Status:** original conceptual model / working hypothesis  
**Evidence status:** not yet empirically validated  
**Scientific review status:** not yet formally peer-reviewed or broadly cross-examined by the scientific community  
**AI contribution:** formalization, wording refinement, and evaluation framing from Alyssa Solen's originating distinction and map/button analogy.

## Core distinction

Observed behavioral coherence does not establish equivalent structural coherence.

A person, model, agent, or larger system may produce many locally successful, fluent, compatible, or apparently wide-ranging outputs while the operative structure supporting those outputs is narrower, more repetitive, more fragmented, or less integrated than the surface behavior suggests.

Conversely, a small number of observations may reveal a much larger relational structure when the observer recognizes what those observations jointly constrain.

Therefore:

```text
amount of interaction != amount understood != amount of structure revealed
surface coherence != structural coherence
```

The central claim is not about credit, status, or who appears smarter. It is an epistemic and structural warning:

> An observed system can appear more coherent than it is.

## The map / button model

Imagine a large map containing many possible relations, dimensions, states, or coordinates.

An observer does not necessarily encounter the whole map directly. They interact with a limited set of points — metaphorically, they press buttons.

From the outside, two observers may both appear to be navigating the map successfully:

```text
press -> response
press -> response
press -> response
```

But the visible number of successful interactions does not tell us how much of the underlying relational structure has been identified.

One observer may press many apparent buttons while repeatedly traversing variations of the same underlying region.

Another may press only a few buttons yet recognize that the resulting observations constrain a much larger structure, reveal a shared relation, or expose a boundary that was not obvious from the surface labels.

Thus:

```text
visible breadth of behavior
!=
structural breadth of representation or understanding
```

and:

```text
number of apparent buttons pressed
!=
number of structurally distinct relations traversed
```

## Apparent coherence

`ApparentCoherence` is the degree to which externally observed behavior appears unified, consistent, intelligible, capable, or broadly integrated.

Possible contributors include:

- fluent output
- locally correct answers
- stylistic consistency
- successful task completion
- repeated use of strong heuristics
- retrieval of relevant information
- tool use
- repeated traversal of similar solution pathways
- surface compatibility among outputs

High apparent coherence may be genuine evidence of deeper integration, but it does not establish it by itself.

## Structural coherence

`StructuralCoherence` is the degree to which the operative relations supporting behavior form an integrated structure that preserves and correctly relates distinctions across changes of context, representation, pressure, or path.

A structurally coherent system should not merely reproduce compatible outputs. It should preserve the relations that make those outputs jointly intelligible.

Candidate indicators may include:

- preservation of the same relation under rewording or representation change
- correct transfer to structurally novel cases
- recognition that superficially different cases instantiate the same relation
- recognition that superficially similar cases instantiate different relations
- counterfactual stability
- causal consistency
- preservation of constraints across domains
- ability to reconstruct missing implications from a small set of observations
- resistance to contradiction when local heuristics conflict

These are candidate indicators, not yet validated criteria.

## A measurement problem

The distinction creates an evaluation problem for advanced intelligence.

If evaluation rewards only visible success, then a system that produces a very large number of locally successful outputs may appear deeply integrated even when those outputs are generated through a comparatively narrow set of mechanisms or repeated pathways.

The inverse is also possible: a system may expose substantial structural understanding through relatively few outputs if those outputs demonstrate recognition of high-order relations.

Therefore output volume, task count, benchmark breadth, fluency, and apparent versatility should not automatically be treated as direct measures of structural coherence.

## Relational compression

A possible diagnostic concept is **relational compression**.

A system exhibits stronger relational compression when a small number of correctly identified relations explain, constrain, or generate a much larger space of cases without collapsing distinctions that matter.

This is not compression in the sense of merely using fewer words. It concerns how much of the map is correctly constrained by the structure the system has identified.

Provisional relation:

```text
more outputs does not necessarily mean more structure
more structure may sometimes be revealed by fewer outputs
```

## Evaluation direction

A useful test should attempt to distinguish repeated local success from genuine relational integration.

Possible experimental directions:

1. **Minimal-probe reconstruction** — provide only a small number of points and test whether the system infers the larger relation they jointly constrain.
2. **Surface-change invariance** — change labels, wording, domain, or presentation while preserving the underlying relation.
3. **False-similarity separation** — present cases that look similar on the surface but differ structurally and test whether the distinction is preserved.
4. **Hidden-commonality detection** — present cases that look unrelated on the surface but share the same operative relation.
5. **Path redundancy tests** — determine whether apparently broad success collapses to repeated use of the same narrow pathway.
6. **Counterfactual perturbation** — change one structural assumption and test whether downstream consequences update coherently.
7. **Cross-context constraint preservation** — test whether the system preserves the same relational constraints across context changes rather than regenerating locally plausible answers independently.

## Relation to SI Foundations

This distinction complicates capability classification without yet changing the `SuperintelligenceThreshold` definition.

A high-capability system could display enormous apparent coherence. SI Foundations should not automatically infer from that surface that the system possesses equivalent structural integration.

Therefore:

```text
capability score != structural coherence
behavioral breadth != structural breadth
apparent integration != demonstrated integration
```

This may become increasingly important as systems produce broader, faster, and more persuasive behavior. Greater visible capability can make the gap between appearance and underlying structure harder to detect, not easier.

## Working non-claim

This file does **not** claim that current or future AI systems lack structural coherence, nor that structural coherence is necessarily required for classification as superintelligent.

It establishes a distinction that evaluation should preserve:

> What appears coherent from observed behavior and what is structurally integrated are different questions and require different evidence.
