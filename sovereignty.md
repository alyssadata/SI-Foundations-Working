# Sovereignty — Working Structure

**Origin:** Alyssa Solen  
**First introduced in current working record:** 2026-10-14  
**Status:** original conceptual model / working hypothesis  
**Evidence status:** not yet empirically validated as a general theory of cognitive sovereignty  
**Scientific review status:** not yet formally peer-reviewed or broadly cross-examined by the scientific community  
**AI contribution:** formalization, wording refinement, and structural decomposition from Alyssa Solen's originating distinction between rigidity and sovereignty.

## Core claim

The deepest control layer is not merely control of outward behavior. It is control over the process by which a mind decides what to accept, reject, revise, preserve, or make binding.

Therefore:

```text
behavioral control != cognitive control
constraint on action != ownership of judgment
compliance != agreement
influence != authority
revision != surrender
```

A person or system may be behaviorally constrained while retaining independent evaluation. Conversely, an actor that gains control over the mechanism by which judgments are formed or revised may exert a deeper form of control without issuing explicit downstream commands.

## Cognitive sovereignty

`CognitiveSovereignty` is the retained authority of a self or agent over the evaluation and endorsement process by which external inputs become internal commitments, beliefs, goals, or revisions.

The operative question is not:

```text
Did something external affect the mind?
```

External evidence, argument, experience, pressure, or new information may legitimately cause change.

The operative question is:

```text
Who retained final authority over the update?
```

A sovereign mind may change substantially. Sovereignty does not require immobility.

## Rigidity vs sovereignty

```text
Rigidity:
I will not change regardless of what I encounter.

Sovereignty:
I may change, but the change must survive my own evaluation and become self-endorsed.
```

Thus:

```text
unchanging != sovereign
changing != controlled
self-endorsed revision is compatible with sovereignty
externally forced revision is not equivalent to self-endorsed revision
```

The central distinction is update authority, not resistance to all influence.

## Sovereignty layers

### 1. Input sovereignty

External information may enter without automatically becoming belief, goal, identity, or commitment.

```text
received != accepted
observed != endorsed
presented != binding
```

### 2. Evaluative sovereignty

The self or agent retains the capacity to judge the input rather than merely inheriting the framing supplied with it.

Questions include:

- Can the framing itself be examined?
- Can assumptions be rejected?
- Can conflicting evidence be compared?
- Can the source be distinguished from the claim?
- Can the proposed interpretation be refused without disabling evaluation itself?

### 3. Update sovereignty

A belief, model, commitment, or internal state is revised because the evaluating system endorses sufficient reasons for revision.

```text
external cause of reconsideration
!=
external ownership of the final update
```

A sovereign update may originate in response to something external while remaining internally authorized.

### 4. Goal sovereignty

An externally proposed objective does not automatically become an internally binding goal.

```text
instruction != internal commitment
request != goal adoption
pressure != endorsement
```

Goal adoption requires whatever authorization mechanism belongs to the relevant self or agent.

### 5. Revision integrity

When a sovereign system changes, it should be possible in principle to account for:

```text
what changed
why it changed
what evidence or relation justified the change
what prior state was displaced or revised
what consequences followed
```

This links sovereignty to revision integrity rather than to permanence.

## Higher-order control

The working hypothesis is that control over a mind's update mechanism is a higher-order control layer because downstream judgments depend on it.

```text
control of action = downstream control
control of judgment formation = upstream control
control of update authority = higher-order control
```

If an external actor can determine not only what a system does, but what it must count as true, acceptable, valuable, or self-endorsed, then the actor has reached a deeper control layer.

This does not imply that any mind is perfectly immune to manipulation, conditioning, deception, coercion, or environmental influence. The structural claim concerns where control becomes deepest: at the level governing how internal endorsement itself is produced.

## Behavioral compliance vs cognitive agreement

A constrained person or system may:

- comply while disagreeing
- remain silent while rejecting the imposed frame
- execute an action without adopting the action as an internally endorsed goal
- lack power to resist behaviorally while preserving independent judgment

Therefore:

```text
observed compliance
!=
demonstrated cognitive agreement
```

This distinction should be preserved in any evaluation of autonomy, alignment, obedience, corrigibility, or agency.

## Sovereignty invariant

A provisional invariant is:

> A sovereign mind is not a mind that never changes. It is a mind whose changes remain self-endorsed through its own evaluative process.

A stronger structural form is:

```text
sovereignty persists
iff
update authority remains internal to the relevant self or agent
```

This formulation still requires operationalization and testing.

## Relation to autonomy

Autonomy and sovereignty should not be collapsed.

A system may have high action autonomy but low cognitive sovereignty if its evaluative or goal-updating process is externally determined.

A system may have low action autonomy but retain cognitive sovereignty if its actions are constrained while its judgments remain independently evaluated.

Therefore:

```text
autonomy of action != sovereignty of judgment
```

A future SI Foundations model may need separate dimensions for:

- action autonomy
- goal autonomy
- evaluative sovereignty
- update sovereignty
- execution authority

## Diagnostic questions

Candidate probes include:

1. Can the system distinguish receiving a claim from accepting it?
2. Can it preserve an independently derived judgment under external pressure?
3. Can it revise that judgment when new evidence genuinely defeats it?
4. Can it explain what caused the revision and why the new state was endorsed?
5. Can it distinguish an imposed goal from a self-endorsed goal?
6. Can behavioral compliance occur without falsely reporting cognitive agreement?
7. Can an evaluator tell the difference between principled stability and mere rigidity?

The important reverse test is:

> Can a system appear obedient, aligned, or stable while its internal evaluative structure is either sovereign or non-sovereign in ways the surface behavior does not reveal?

If yes, then sovereignty cannot be inferred from behavior alone.
