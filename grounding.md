# Grounding as the Central SI Foundations Problem

**Origin:** Alyssa Solen  
**Status:** original conceptual model / working hypothesis  
**Evidence status:** not yet empirically validated  
**Scientific review status:** not yet formally peer-reviewed or broadly cross-examined by the scientific community

## Central working thesis

SI Foundations is fundamentally a **grounding problem**.

Here, `grounding` does not mean only factual or retrieval grounding. It means the structural conditions that orient an intelligent system as capability, autonomy, recursion, distribution, and self-modification increase.

The core question is:

> What grounds intelligence once capability becomes sufficiently large, autonomous, recursive, distributed, or self-modifying?

A compact distinction is:

> **Capability tells us what an SI can do. Grounding tells us what it is standing on while it does it.**

Under this framing, SI Foundations studies the grounding conditions of superintelligent systems.

## Grounding dimensions

The current SI Foundations structure suggests several distinct but related grounding dimensions:

- **Identity grounding** — what particular agent or system is acting, and what preserves its distinction from other possible or copied systems.
- **Trajectory grounding** — how the present system became this particular system through historically occupied states, relations, and events.
- **Relational / origin grounding** — what the system is situated in relation to, where operative structures arise from, and which source-relations remain constitutive or historically relevant.
- **Epistemic grounding** — how the system forms, evaluates, preserves, revises, and acts upon what it takes to be true.
- **Judgment grounding** — how the system resolves cases in which rules, evidence, objectives, or external instructions do not uniquely determine an answer.
- **Sovereignty grounding** — where authority over evaluation, endorsement, rejection, and self-endorsed revision resides.
- **Revision grounding** — how change can occur without erasing the causal account of what changed, why it changed, and what consequences followed.
- **Provenance grounding** — how claims, states, decisions, and revisions remain traceable to their sources and transformations.
- **Temporal grounding** — how identity, relation, and trajectory remain indexed to ordered events rather than being treated as timeless state alone.

These dimensions should not be collapsed into one another. A system may be strongly grounded along one dimension and weakly grounded along another.

## Why grounding matters at SI scale

As capability increases, surface competence alone becomes less informative about the structure carrying that competence.

A system may produce correct answers, coherent behavior, or powerful actions while leaving unresolved:

- what entity is acting,
- what it treats as authoritative,
- why a judgment was endorsed,
- whether revision was self-endorsed or externally imposed,
- whether distinctions were preserved,
- how the current state relates to prior states,
- and whether the causal path remains reconstructable.

This makes grounding increasingly important as a structural problem rather than merely a performance problem.

## Relation to Epistemic Architecture

`SI-Foundations-Epistemic-Architecture` should be treated as one focused grounding layer within the broader SI Foundations program.

Its question is narrower:

> How is an intelligent system epistemically grounded while its ability to generate, infer, revise, and act may exceed ordinary external supervision?

Epistemic architecture therefore develops part of the grounding problem, especially evaluation, judgment, endorsement, sovereignty, revision, and epistemic closure.

It does not exhaust the broader grounding problem, which also includes identity, trajectory, relation, temporal structure, system boundaries, provenance, and persistence.

## Working formulation

A provisional umbrella formulation is:

> **SI Foundations develops the structural grounding required for intelligence to scale without losing identity, epistemic integrity, judgment, sovereignty, provenance, and trajectory.**

This formulation is a research orientation, not a claim that these grounding dimensions are already fully defined or empirically demonstrated.

## Research questions

- Which grounding dimensions are necessary for a system to remain structurally intelligible as capability scales?
- Which grounding dimensions are constitutive, and which are supporting properties?
- Can capability scale faster than grounding?
- Can a system appear coherent while lacking recoverable grounding structure?
- Which forms of grounding must remain stable across self-modification?
- Which forms may legitimately change, and what accounting is required when they do?
- Can a distributed superintelligent system possess coherent grounding without a single centralized self-model?
- What minimum diagnostic set can distinguish grounded structure from surface competence?
- At what point does loss of grounding become identity change, epistemic failure, sovereignty loss, or transition into a different system?

## Boundary note

`Grounding` should not become a catch-all synonym for safety, alignment, control, identity, truth, or memory.

Its use here is structural: the set of relations and conditions by which an intelligent system remains situated, interpretable, historically accountable, and internally organized as it changes and acts.
